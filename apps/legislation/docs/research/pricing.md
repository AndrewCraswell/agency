The [pricing and offerings](../product/pricing.md) page owns the revised proposal. Response/query/issue quotas, top-ups,
included seat counts and a fixed enterprise floor from the initial report are superseded or undecided; they are not retained as current offerings.

## Scope and evidence

The market review covers 14 directly relevant legislative tracking or broader policy intelligence products, three adjacent legal research/AI platforms, and the Plural Open free-data alternative. This is a broad U.S.-focused competitive set, not an exhaustive inventory of every regional service, legal application, or emerging startup. International, advocacy-only, practice-management, and contract-only products are outside the primary comparison.

Prices are in USD, checked September 14, 2026. Published amounts are distinguished from custom quotes and recommendations. Annual prices divided by 12 are equivalents, not monthly cancellation rights. Tax, negotiated discounts, and unlisted add-ons are excluded. Product features below are vendor representations; commercial subscriptions were not tested for accuracy, uptime, or complete coverage. A missing public price means a comparable quote was not obtained, not that the service is necessarily expensive.

BillTrack50 and LawSignals were checked in the live browser. Their current pages differed from search-index or extracted snapshots. Live terms take precedence. Other entries use official product and pricing pages, with Bloomberg Government limited to indexed official-page evidence. No vendor was contacted.

Tabra's baseline comes from its current repository specification, product backlog, source files, and latest dated release notes. This is not a new production acceptance audit. The distinction matters because substantial backend work exists while the customer-facing product remains under development.

Plural pricing and Quorum quote-based packaging were rechecked during consolidation. Other entries retain the initial review's date and verification method; they were not all rechecked during the docs audit.

## Feature overlap and the opportunity

| Customer job | Market evidence | Tabra's intended position | Commercial conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Find a bill and inspect basic facts | Available from free and paid tracking products [2][3][7][20] | Canonical records and official source links | Free foundation |
| Follow a list of bills | Generous free monitoring already exists [7] | Personal follows and configurable notifications | Charge for richer ongoing work, not simply a small bill-count increase |
| Ask AI about legislation | Plural, FastDemocracy, PolicyNote and others advertise AI [4][7][10] | Source-grounded conversation with inspectable evidence | AI alone is not distinctive |
| Compare revisions | Multiple products advertise comparison [4][10][11][12] | Exact versions, passages and context retained with findings | Win on reproducibility, clarity and task completion; benchmark this directly |
| Prepare client advice inputs | Reporting and sharing are widespread [3][5][11] | Reviewed brief with dates, limitations and citations | Strong paid workflow if it saves real preparation and review time |
| Coordinate multiple people | Available in team tracking and legal AI platforms [3][4][19] | Currently deferred | Required for Firm; baseline collaboration cannot alone justify enterprise pricing |
| Research everything affecting current law | Regulatory/code/legal platforms offer broader corpora [11][12][18] | Deliberately narrower legislative corpus | Sell to legislative monitoring practices; qualify out comprehensive-law requirements |
| Connect to external AI or internal tools | PolicyNote has MCP/API; other competitors expose APIs [3][10][11] | API-backed MCP and shared services | Useful distribution and integration route, not a unique moat |
| Meet enterprise governance needs | SSO, audit, lifecycle and related controls appear in enterprise products [5][19] | Additional implementation and operational proof needed | Enterprise price must pay for administration, control and dependable service |

Tabra's most promising differentiation is the combination of inspectable passages, pinned versions, visible coverage gaps, persistent issue context, and easy review. These are product commitments to prove, not claims that competitors lack evidence or that Tabra is more accurate. Run the same research task in competing products before making comparative claims.

## Implications for the revised offering

Unlimited tracking and organization-wide access are already offered in parts of the market. They support the decision
to start with flat feature-based pricing. Some products meter practice trackers or API results, but those meters are not
evidence that Tabra must cap user questions or bills. Do not compare a vendor's developer-data subscription with its app plan.

The initial mockup review inspected 18 frames: cited answers, pinned document versions, comparisons, personal issues,
following, civic records and brief previews. The housing issue contains several saved queries; this is a workflow detail,
not a reason to introduce quotas. Shared client work, report approval and branded Word/PDF exports remain proposed additions.

Professional sells repeatable research. Firm sells shared ownership and reviewed deliverables. Enterprise sells proven
administration and contracted service. Regulatory/current-code coverage, expert screening, contacts, transcripts,
advocacy and international content remain meaningful competitor differences; adding organization features does not erase them.

Validate the $79/$299 hypotheses with completed customer tasks and paid pilots. Compare time to a reviewable brief,
citation correctness, missed developments, reviewer effort and actual account cost. No demand curve or complete operating
cost distribution has been measured. Do not claim price parity implies feature or data parity.
