# Competitors by ideal customer profile

## 1. Start with the buying job

September 15, 2026. **The competitive set depends on the ICP, the task and the purchasing scope.** This is not a ranking
of legislative software in general. Use the segment definitions in [ICPs](icp.md) first, then identify what that prospect
actually uses and what purchase Tabra would replace or supplement. A vendor can be a direct substitute for one job,
an adjacent budget competitor for another, and irrelevant to a third.

Our recommended initial focus is association policy research and solo/boutique public-affairs research. Their main
alternatives are shared trackers, low-cost individual trackers, public sources plus office tools, and existing policy
subscriptions. Comprehensive legal research and public-affairs CRM become primary competition only when the buyer needs
those broader jobs. Do not force every ICP into a Quorum-versus-Tabra comparison.

### Evidence standard

- **Refreshed:** the official public page was retrieved September 15, 2026. Capabilities and prices remain vendor
  representations, not authenticated product tests or independent accuracy/uptime evidence.
- **Retained:** the September 14 [pricing research](../research/pricing.md) records official evidence, but this review
  did not refresh it successfully or did not revisit the page. Treat it as dated evidence and recheck before a sales claim.
- **Hypothesis:** our inference about relevance, switching difficulty or potential advantage. No actual prospect
  shortlist, win/loss history, competitor customer economics or head-to-head study was supplied.

Tabra is assessed as documented services plus a developing product/design, **not a shipped equivalent to every listed

## 3. Primary ICP battlecards

### A. Trade associations and professional bodies

**Buying decision:** produce reliable member/board legislative updates with a small policy team.

| Alternative and strength | Gap relative to Tabra | Recommended response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BillTrack50: shared sheets, custom reports, widgets, unlimited users and API access [S1] | Our shared research, distribution and organization access are not yet delivered equivalents | Win a narrow research-preparation task first; do not promise to replace member-facing widgets or distribution |
| Plural: paid team AI/comparisons, positions/notes/assignments and shared issue exports [S2] | Direct overlap with our planned research/collaboration; chat and issue tracking alone do not distinguish us | Benchmark source inspection, relevance review and a repeatable briefing task |
| FastDemocracy: reporting, hearings/transcripts, staff contacts, mobile and outreach [S3] | Broader operational workflow and source types than our current personal scope | Pursue research-heavy teams; decline full replacement when live hearings/outreach are essential |
| StateScape or GovHawk: screened tracking/support or broader codes/regulations [S9][S10] | Human screening, service and corpus breadth are real missing capabilities for a buyer who relies on them | Offer a supplement only when self-directed legislative research remains useful |

**Potential advantage to prove:** an understandable issue-to-reviewed-brief process with exact versions and less
preparation/reviewer effort. Competitors may already provide good evidence links; do not claim otherwise.
**Proof task:** one real public member issue, two relevant jurisdictions, an amended provision, an excluded false match,
and a finished cited update. Compare active work time, corrections, missed evidence and ongoing maintenance.
**Switching burden:** saved searches, tags, staff habits, public widgets and existing contract term. Prefer a bounded
parallel pilot; do not insist on migration before value is demonstrated. Member distribution is a separate buying job.

### B. Independent lobbyists and boutique public-affairs researchers

**Buying decision:** answer a client question or prepare a recurring client update faster without weakening evidence.

| Alternative and strength | Gap relative to Tabra | Recommended response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LegiScan: inexpensive single-jurisdiction/professional tracking [S4, retained] | Basic tracking can cost substantially less than our Professional proposal | Sell reviewed research value, not bill-count capacity or a generic cheaper-tracker claim |
| Plural Essential: unlimited tracking and alerts for an individual [S2] | Lower listed annual price, though no AI/collaboration in Essential | Compare the actual research job and needed features, not just seat price |
| FastDemocracy: hearings, contacts, reporting, mobile and client/team tools [S3] | We do not replace the consultant's whole operating toolkit | Lead with an evidence-research supplement; retain their contact/outreach tools |
| Quorum: stakeholder/advocacy and policy modules [S6] | Established broad workflow represented publicly; our contact and campaign scope is deliberately narrower | Qualify out CRM-led buying rather than expanding the roadmap to win one prospect |

**Potential advantage to prove:** low-setup, explicit-scope research that ends in reusable checked findings. MCP may fit
an existing AI habit, but it is not exclusive to Tabra. **Proof task:** unfamiliar-session bill question -> amendment
inspection -> cited client update; include the review step and setup time.
**Switching burden:** personal saved work is lighter than migrating a firm, but confidential client notes make shared
adoption materially harder. A solo subscription does not grant safe firm-wide sharing. Price discovery must distinguish
the owner buying one research tool from partners purchasing a client-workspace platform.

### C. Funded advocacy nonprofits, coalitions and unions

**Buying decision:** maintain an evidence-based issue position and keep the policy team informed.

Free tracking and official sites set the baseline. FastDemocracy's free offer includes unlimited tracked bills and
daily/weekly email; its professional offer adds reporting, team work and outreach [S3]. BillTrack50's shared account and
widgets [S1] compete for research/member communication; Quorum Grassroots competes for mobilization [S6]. These are not
interchangeable purchases.

Our opportunity is funded self-directed research with persistent evidence and manageable relevance review. Our gap is
campaign execution, public widgets and operational support where those are essential. **Win** by proving a better
recurring evidence update; **supplement** an existing campaign platform; **decline** a mass-outreach replacement request.
Test one campaign topic with several irrelevant matches and changed source text. Measure researcher/reviewer labor and
budget authority, not the number of supporters in the organization's mailing list.

## 4. Conditional and later ICP battlecards

### D. In-house government affairs/public policy

Quorum's policy, stakeholder, data and services modules [S6], PolicyNote's research/monitoring and analyst offerings
[S7, retained], GovHawk's legislation/regulations/codes and support [S9], State Net's broader research integration
[S8, retained], and Plural's team/enterprise workflows [S2] form the likely policy shortlist. POLITICO Pro and Bloomberg
Government compete when purchased journalism/analysis is central [S12][S13, retained]. Abstract's workflow execution
is relevant when the buyer wants internal-policy/regulatory automation rather than a research interface [S14].

Their breadth, support and governance expectations are advantages against our developing offering. Tabra's opportunity
is a narrow team-level research task, with a sponsor willing to use it alongside the incumbent. Confirm vendor/security
approval before treating a low-touch trial as realistic. A focused legislative demo cannot establish complete company
compliance, certified controls, an SLA or integration parity. Test the complete leadership-brief workflow and procurement
effort; at our current Firm price a long enterprise sale may not recover acquisition costs.

### E. Specialist legislative/public-policy legal practice

LawSignals is especially relevant when the lawyer wants one of its curated practice trackers and a monthly client-ready
report, not open-ended legislative exploration [S5]. Its currently listed topics include AI, privacy, cybersecurity, IP,
children's online safety and cannabis; confirm topic fit rather than assuming it serves every legal practice.
State Net is relevant when proposed legislation must connect to broader statutory/regulatory research [S8, retained].
Westlaw and Lexis+ compete for legal authorities and existing budget [S15][S16, retained]; Harvey competes for collaborative
legal/document workflows [S17]. They are not all direct substitutes for a bill tracker.

Our proposed-law evidence journey can complement the firm's current subscriptions. It does not supply a citator,
comprehensive operative-law coverage, litigation workflow or privileged document workspace merely because it has sources
and chat. **Proof task:** verify an amendment against two exact versions and produce a qualified, cited advice input.
Measure missing qualifiers and attorney corrections, not the fluency of the generated answer. Decline replacing general
legal research; compare LawSignals directly only for a matching practice/topic and output.

### F. Specialist journalists and newsrooms

Official sources and free trackers compete for source discovery. State Affairs combines statehouse reporting with a
separate Pro tracking/hearing/transcript product [S11]. POLITICO Pro combines reporting, analysis, tracking and workflow
[S12]; Bloomberg Government is a retained news/analysis/tracking reference, not a freshly tested product [S13].

Our opportunity is an efficient verification and historical-evidence supplement. Exclusive reporting, sources/contacts,
live transcripts and newsroom expertise are competitor strengths we should not claim to replace. Test verification of
a public bill/vote claim and direct source access for the resulting story. A newsroom's interest or potential exposure
does not establish recurring subscription revenue; keep editorial independence separate from commercial outreach.

### G. Academic and think-tank research

The Open States site currently emphasizes API-key management and bulk/API access, directing free bill/legislator search
to Plural Open [S18]. Official datasets, LegiScan API [S19, retained] and internal research pipelines can be better suited
to large, reproducible quantitative cohorts than an interactive chat product.

Tabra may reduce work on qualitative evidence gathering and version comparison. It has not established full historical
population coverage or unrestricted redistribution rights. **Proof task:** reproduce a scoped finding with stable source
versions and explicit missing-data treatment. Compare to the researcher's existing extraction method. Decline a required
full-corpus data export until rights and dataset completeness are agreed; do not count a free upstream ecosystem as a
second independent paid company in market sizing.

### H. Legislative staff and other public offices

The relevant baseline is often the office's own system and official sources. LegiScan/BillTrack50 can be tracking
alternatives [S1][S4, retained], while State Net or broader policy suites enter when cross-jurisdiction research, integration
or agency-wide monitoring is required. FastDemocracy explicitly markets public-office workflows [S3]; that representation
does not establish acceptance under a particular agency's procurement rules.

Our possible advantage is simpler external-jurisdiction research. Procurement, accessibility evidence, retention,
authorized identity and ongoing support are substantive gaps to close, not UI extras. Test an authorized comparative
memo task with an office sponsor and the actual purchasing constraints. Do not infer budget size from government status
or compete to replace internal legislative drafting systems without explicit demand and scope approval.

### I. Developers, internal AI/data teams and civic-tech products

LegiScan API [S19, retained], Open States [S18], official-source acquisition, and PolicyNote API/MCP [S7][S20, retained]
are the relevant integration baseline. Quorum's Enterprise Data [S6] and GovHawk's custom feeds [S9] enter for broader
licensed content. Abstract [S14] is more directly relevant if buying executed business workflows instead of raw evidence.

Tabra's intended advantage is a consistent canonical service and evidence model across source systems. Prove that through
an authenticated integration, stable IDs, version-specific citations, correction/deletion handling and reliable pagination.
MCP is not a monopoly, and data already accessible upstream is not automatically proprietary advantage. Rights,
freshness/service commitments and support economics may dominate the decision. Compare total maintenance cost of
build-versus-buy; no consumer plan price implies unlimited downstream serving or redistribution.

### J. Citizens and occasional personal research

Free official sites and free tracking are the main alternatives. FastDemocracy, BillTrack50 and Plural Open offer
discovery/individual entry paths [S1][S3][S18]; LegiScan is also a retained free/low-cost benchmark [S4]. Search engines
and an existing AI subscription may satisfy occasional explanation needs without another purchase.

Tabra should compete on understandable, verifiable discovery in its free experience, not a paid bill-count increase.
Verify representative identity and procedural-vote understanding, but do not project consumer conversion from usability.
Professional reporting, client workspaces and delivery diagnostics are not reasons for this user to pay. This segment
should not set the initial paid acquisition budget or require turning Tabra into a broad political news product.

### What the price comparisons actually imply

- For a single-jurisdiction association, BillTrack50's listed $1,000 shared subscription is below our proposed $2,990
  Firm price. Do not market Tabra as universally cheaper or charge for unused national coverage as though it creates value.
- For a nationwide team, $2,990 is $2,010 below the $5,000 headline offers, but our seats, shipped workflows, rights and
  service are not established equivalents. Price difference is not evidence of a competitive advantage or sustainable margin.
- For a solo researcher, Tabra's $790 annual proposal exceeds Plural Essential's $609 and LawSignals Starter's $468;
  the task and included features differ. The extra cost needs demonstrated research utility, not an AI label.
- Free unlimited tracking means ordinary bill following is a weak paid proposition. Reports, evidence work and recurring
  organization jobs must support our price; no quotas/top-ups are implied by competitors' meters.
- Compare the buyer's incremental cost. If a legal/policy suite is already budgeted, its relevant tools may cost little
  extra. A supplemental Tabra purchase must save enough work to justify another subscription and onboarding.

For quotes, hold constant ICP, user/recipient counts, jurisdictions, historical depth, source types, research/report
requirements, API/redistribution rights, service, contract length and renewal/cancellation terms. Do not compare a
service-rich research team or legal library to a software-only prototype without identifying the missing work.

## 7. How competition changes our focus

1. **Start with an unmet research-output job, not a tracker replacement.** Associations and boutiques are plausible
   only when their current tools leave material preparation/verification work. Someone satisfied with a $100 tracker
   has no reason to pay us merely for a different interface.
2. **Use current buyer relationships to choose the first vertical.** Where a channel partner/referral network already
   exists, reachable qualified buyers may outweigh a theoretical larger market. Confirm need and coverage together.
3. **Do not build feature breadth to avoid qualification.** News, expert screening, CRM, transcripts or legal citators
   can be indispensable to a customer without becoming our next deliverable.
4. **Pair solo adoption with a credible organization path.** Solos offer shorter decision chains but lower account
   revenue; association/boutique team expansion needs delivered privacy and shared reports, not a pricing-page upgrade.
5. **Keep enterprise and developer economics separate.** No higher ACV is assumed until rights, service and procurement
   are priced. Use the [ICP contribution/CAC model](icp.md#5-revenue-earning-potential-and-acquisition-constraints), not
   an unverified expensive-incumbent narrative.

## 8. Evidence to collect before making comparative claims

For each qualified ICP pilot, record the actual incumbent/package, scope, users, existing cost, renewal date, indispensable
features and switching objections. Identify whether we replace, supplement, or lose to doing nothing. Record facts only
with the prospect's permission; do not seek confidential competitor contracts or private client material for a public demo.

Use the same public task and source truth set in authorized competitor trials and Tabra: discover scoped relevant bills,
exclude false matches, compare exact versions, inspect a procedural vote, handle missing evidence, prepare a cited update,
and repeat after a source change. Add alerts or shared review only for an ICP that needs them and a product that supports them.

Measure setup time, active researcher time, corrections/reviewer effort, missed/false matches, version/citation correctness,
copy/report usefulness, recurrence and support burden. Distinguish source-publication delay, ingestion delay and message
delivery. Report which plan/data scope was tested and how recently. No benchmark should claim nationwide superiority
from one bill or treat vendor-published accuracy/satisfaction statistics as independently established results.

Keep findings with the owning research evidence and update these two documents. A competitor gap becomes a build
priority only when repeated qualified buyers require it, our costs/rights permit it, and it improves expected retained
contribution. Otherwise qualify out or position as a supplement. Revisit focus after paid conversion and renewal evidence,
not after adding another vendor to a feature spreadsheet.
