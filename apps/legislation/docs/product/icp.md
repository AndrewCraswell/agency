# Ideal customer profiles and opportunity strategy

## 1. Recommendation and evidence status

September 15, 2026. **Focus first on lean trade-association policy teams and independent/boutique public-affairs
researchers with recurring, source-backed legislative reporting work in verified jurisdictions.** Use individual
researchers as the lower-friction entry point; pursue organization expansion only when shared ownership and isolation
are delivered. Selective advocacy organizations are the next cohort. Do not lead with enterprise compliance or replace
a customer's sole critical-alert system before reliability acceptance.

This is a commercial hypothesis, not validated demand. It evaluates the [product specification](product-spec.md),
the designed research/issue/monitoring journeys, [pricing proposals](pricing.md), and dated implementation evidence.
[Competitors](competitors.md) owns the competitive comparison; the pricing document owns commercial terms.
No interviews, paid conversions, willingness-to-pay study, current customer counts or measured acquisition costs
were supplied. Marketability, fit and priority judgments below must be tested, not repeated as market facts.

An ICP describes a buying organization or individual with a repeated problem, budget and adoption conditions. A persona
describes a user inside that account. A lawyer, analyst or citizen is not an ICP merely because they can use the app.

Choose the ICP and buying job before the competitor shortlist. Record whether we replace a tracker, supplement a
research suite, or compete with manual work; vendors are not universally direct competitors. These ten profiles define
the opportunity space, not ten simultaneous go-to-market programs.

### The job we should sell

Find relevant proposed legislation, verify precisely what changed, maintain an investigation, and prepare an update
that another person can check against the source. The repeatable loop is **find -> verify -> organize -> monitor -> brief**.
AI chat, bill counts and database size are means to that outcome, not sufficient paid differentiation.

### Common qualification

- A named person researches legislation at least weekly during active periods and produces a recurring deliverable.
- The account can name its issues, jurisdictions, relevant sessions and required source types.
- Someone owns the accuracy/review of the output and has authority to evaluate or purchase the tool.
- Current work involves repetitive source navigation, version checking, spreadsheet curation or briefing preparation.
- The prospect accepts a bounded research pilot with official-source verification; it does not require unsupported guarantees.
- The pilot does not depend on a missing feature that is the customer's primary reason to buy.

The team-size ranges below are prospecting hypotheses about research users, not approved seat bundles or quotas.
"Not central" means do not build/package it for this initial job; it does not claim nobody in that segment ever needs it.
"Required gap" means qualify out or wait when that capability is essential, rather than dismissing the customer's need.

Ratings are explicit judgments, not measured market statistics. Revenue means account potential at our proposed offer,
not segment TAM. Acquisition difficulty includes finding a buyer, getting trust/approval, onboarding and switching costs.

| J. Citizens | Episodic | None assumed | High per paid conversion | Low UI, potentially costly usage | Free audience, not paid ICP |

### Focus to maximize earnings relative to acquisition effort

No data currently proves which segment maximizes profit. The best current hypothesis is **association policy teams for
account economics, alongside solos/boutiques for speed of learning and early individual adoption**. Associations have a
repeatable reporting obligation and recognizable buyers; solos can decide without an enterprise buying committee.
Neither advantage establishes low CAC until tested.

Narrow those cohorts further by the competitive job. A single-state association satisfied with low-cost shared tracking
is less attractive than a policy team doing substantial cross-jurisdiction verification and briefing work. A solo who
only wants free alerts is not the same opportunity as one selling recurring client research. Conversely, an association
that requires public widgets or outsourced screening is a weaker fit despite its apparent budget. The recommendation
should change if existing relationships make another qualified cohort materially easier to reach and serve profitably.

Do not prioritize in-house enterprise merely because its budget could be larger. At today's proposed Firm price, an
enterprise procurement/support process can make the account unattractive. Do not prioritize citizens merely because
signup is easy: low purchase friction and low customer acquisition cost are different things.

Maintain one product workflow across these cohorts. Choose one public issue vertical and a few demonstrably supported
jurisdictions for the pilot, using buyer access, pain and coverage together. Housing, employment, energy or professional
licensing are candidates, not a decision based on housing mockups. Do not pick the first market solely from the easiest
scraper or promise cross-state completeness from isolated ingestion successes.

## 5. Revenue, earning potential and acquisition constraints

### Model assumptions

Use current proposal only: Professional $79 monthly or $790 annually; Firm $299 monthly or $2,990 annually.
Annual contracts normalize to $65.83 and $249.17 monthly revenue respectively, not the monthly-cancel price.
Firm included seats, API rights and enterprise terms remain undecided. No account expansion or seat overage is modeled.
All amounts are USD before tax; discounts, churn, collection failure and costs are not included in the revenue scenarios.

| Steady-state paid cohort, assuming annual plans | Annualized recurring revenue | What this does not prove |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 100 Professional accounts | $79,000 | Number of reachable/buying professionals or their retention |
| 100 Firm organizations | $299,000 | Delivered firm capabilities, approved seat economics or conversion |
| 200 Professional + 50 Firm organizations | $307,500 | That the accounts can be acquired within any time period |
| 100 Professional + 200 Firm organizations | $677,000 | Profitability or a viable sales/support organization |

These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts, TAM/SAM/SOM, or launch commitments. Monthly plans annualize to $948 and
$3,588 before churn; do not mix those figures with annual-plan cohorts. Count a solo upgraded to Firm once, not as both.
About 3.78 Professional annual accounts equal one Firm annual account in revenue, before their different costs.

### Profitability and CAC sensitivity

Measure contribution after serving the account: model/retrieval, message delivery, variable storage, payment processing,
support and onboarding allocation. Fixed/shared corpus acquisition, engineering and general overhead still have to be paid.
No gross/contribution margin has been measured; the following 60%/80% cases are sensitivity assumptions, not claims.

| Annual plan | Monthly revenue equivalent | Monthly contribution at 60%-80% | Acquisition spend recoverable in 6 months at those margins |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Professional | $65.83 | $39.50-$52.67 | $237-$316 |
| Firm | $249.17 | $149.50-$199.33 | $897-$1,196 |

This assumes six months of retained paid service, no expansion, and servicing costs already reflected in margin.
Upfront annual cash is not immediate profit. CAC includes founder/sales time, tools, media, demos and acquisition-related
onboarding; avoid counting the same onboarding expense in both CAC and serving costs. At an illustrative $75/hour fully
loaded acquisition rate, the six-month budget is roughly 3-4 hours per Professional win versus 12-16 hours per Firm win
before any paid channel costs. Those hours are planning constraints, not observed sales-cycle lengths.

Track first-year contribution = collected annual revenue minus serving costs minus acquisition cost. Longer-run earnings
need observed renewal/churn, not an assumed perpetual subscription. For an unpriced developer/enterprise opportunity,
estimate its actual delivery/support cost and negotiate a scoped contract before assigning expected revenue.

### Addressable opportunity research still needed

Build a named, deduplicated account list per cohort with policy staff, public issue remit, budget-owner role, jurisdiction
needs and an authorized outreach route. Use legitimate public business information and opt-in/referral channels; do not
equate directory entries, lobbyist registrations, association members or attorney headcount with purchasable accounts.
SAM requires demonstrated coverage/workflow fit; SOM requires measured reach, conversion, service capacity and retention.
Do not publish numerical market size from the account arithmetic above.

## 6. Competitor implications and product priorities

Basic bill tracking, AI summaries, comparisons and API/MCP are already represented in the competitive set. Our
opportunity is a better verified research-to-brief experience, not a claim that competitors cannot cite sources.
See [Competitors](competitors.md) for vendor evidence, pricing confidence and segment-specific alternatives.

| Priority | Close this gap | Why it affects the target ICP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Source-backed coverage/freshness and search acceptance for chosen pilot jurisdictions | No workflow polish compensates for missed or wrong evidence |
| First | Complete personal issue -> exact comparison -> reviewed/copyable brief | Creates a paid reason beyond free tracking and generic chat |
| First | Tested notification control and recovery | Professional retention depends on trust; do not sell a critical-alert guarantee early |
| Next | Shared ownership, isolation, review and controlled reports | Unlocks association/boutique organization economics rather than only personal seats |
| Conditional | API packaging, rights, docs and service envelope | Enables developer business if demand/support economics justify it |
| Later | Regulatory/current-law coverage and enterprise controls | Serves broader legal/compliance/enterprise needs, but is a different readiness burden |

Do not chase CRM/PAC, exclusive political journalism, court citators, mass advocacy or expert research services simply
to match a competitor checklist. Those can be reasons to decline a prospect. Reconsider only with repeat paid demand,
cost/rights evidence, and an explicit scope decision.

## 7. Validation and decision rules

Recommended experiment, not an executed campaign: interview 10 association buyers/users, 6 solo/boutique researchers,
and 4 funded advocacy teams, then invite qualified accounts to a paid or explicitly priced pilot when the necessary
workflow is ready. Keep interviews distinct from product demos; ask about the last real update, tools, review time,
missed developments, budget owner, contract dates and indispensable features before presenting our solution.

For each candidate, walk a public, non-sensitive issue from source discovery to reviewed brief. Record baseline active
work time, Rostra-assisted time, corrections/reviewer effort, coverage failures, repeat use, support time and purchase
decision at the disclosed price. Set acceptable accuracy/missed-event criteria with the prospect before testing. Do not
interpret enthusiasm, free usage, or a letter of intent as recognized revenue or validated renewal.

Proposed go/no-go thresholds to test: a recurring weekly use case, a named buyer, zero critical citation/version errors
in the reviewed pilot, meaningful time saved without extra reviewer cleanup, and acquisition/service effort inside the
chosen payback budget. A starting time-saving target of 30% is a pilot hypothesis, not a marketing claim.
Stop/reshape a segment when most prospects require missing primary capabilities, the price fails to cover support,
or the task is only occasional. Increase focus when paid conversion, repeated work and renewal evidence agree.

Marketing tests should measure the funnel by segment: qualified accounts reached -> discovery meetings -> coverage-fit
pilots -> paid accounts -> active recurring output -> renewal. Compare founder hours and total acquisition spend per
retained account, not clicks or trial signups. No sales-cycle, CAC, conversion or churn benchmark is assumed here.

## 8. Ownership and update policy

Product/GTM owns ICP qualification, experiments and segment focus; engineering/data owns coverage and service evidence;
privacy/legal reviews source rights, outreach and shared-data commitments. Pricing owns all tier changes. Update these
profiles when paid-pilot evidence changes the recommendation, not when a new mockup makes an unsupported segment look ready.
Until then, use this as a disciplined prospecting and prioritization plan, not a public assertion of product-market fit.
